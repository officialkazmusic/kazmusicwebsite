MAKASALANAN EP

Release Date: August 12,2023

Listen on Spotify:

Shifting focus from personal expression to sharp societal critique, Makasalanan EP marks a major evolutionary leap for KAZ, presenting a significantly more mature, technical, and deliberate iteration of the duo. Where their debut project laid down their foundational thoughts, Makasalanan directly confronts systemic decay, exploring themes of sin, deceit, corruption, and systemic hypocrisy. Across the tracklist, Zy and unknown¿ dismantle the hidden mechanics of human wrongdoing—targeting religious hypocrisy, political manipulation, state-level greed, and the unchecked personal vices that drive individual pitfalls. It is an uncompromised, high-concept body of work that cements KAZ not just as lyrical commentators, but as serious critics of modern society.

TRACK LIST :

1. Makasalanan

Serving as the title track and heavy-hitting opener for the EP, "Makasalanan" sets the tone with a sampled audio clip of a street preacher warning about blind leaders steering their followers into a "hukay"—or pitfall of demise. This sample directly reinforces the song's catchy yet incisive chorus, which centers on the timeless allegory of the blind leading the blind to explain why so many people end up spiritually and morally astray.
In their signature blunt and direct style, Zy and unknown¿ dismantle religious hypocrisy, calling out individuals who adopt a "holier-than-thou" stance while practicing the very sins they preach against. The track delivers a sharp reality check: hiding behind a veneer of righteousness does not separate the self-proclaimed holy from the sinners they condemn.

2. Pandama

Functioning as an intentional sonic breather on the project, "Pandama" serves as the EP's most accessible and least heavy track. Here, KAZ steps outside their comfort zone, experimenting with a contemporary songwriting style paired with a modern, forward-thinking instrumental from unknown¿—moving away from their signature boom-bap aesthetic. Grounded in the theme of lust, the track cleverly plays on the concept of pandama (senses), using sensory imagery to explore physical attraction, temptation, and the subtle ways desires overtake reason.

3. Sinungaling

Opening with a return to the sampled street preacher from the title track, "Sinungaling" begins with an emphatic warning that liars are bound for damnation. However, KAZ flips this religious premise to deliver a biting critique of state-level corruption and the harsh daily realities facing everyday Filipinos. The track's concept revolves around a clever inversion of the classic Filipino proverb "Ang sinungaling ay kapatid ng magnanakaw" ("A liar is the sibling of a thief"). Rather than focusing on common deceit, Zy and unknown¿ weaponize the saying to expose how political deception and institutional dishonesty directly facilitate systemic theft—demonstrating that in the realm of governance, lie and corruption are inextricably linked.

4. Inosente

Tackling the perils of greed, wealth, and power, "Inosente" approaches its heavy subject matter through the perspective of a naive observer. The track explores society's tendency to idealize the opulent, vice-ridden lifestyles of celebrities and the ultra-rich, striving for the same status without recognizing the hidden costs and moral compromises required to sustain it. Rather than delivering a rigid moral lecture against worldly desires, Zy and unknown¿ craft the song's narrative to prompt genuine reflection—leaving listeners with a poignant rhetorical question about whether material gain and surface-level pleasures are truly all that matter.
